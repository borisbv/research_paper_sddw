**Abstract.** This article proposes a strategic framework for developing a virtual museum platform for the anonymous Art Museum (MAM), using the Brook Stevens archival collection as an initial implementation phase. The objective is to expand public access to MAM’s collections while supporting preservation, education, accessibility, and community engagement. The methodology uses a mixed-methods approach across six stages: stakeholder needs assessment, comparative analysis of virtual museum models, technical framework design, prototype development, user testing, and qualitative and quantitative evaluation. Discussion focuses on how immersive and participatory technologies can democratize access to cultural heritage while preserving curatorial integrity and ensuring inclusive user experiences. The preliminary results from the first three stages identify the need for scalable digital infrastructure, metadata standards, XR integration, and accessibility-centered design. Expected outcomes include increased digital engagement, improved educational resources, stronger community participation, and a sustainable model positioning MAM as a leader in digital cultural heritage innovation.

**Keywords: Virtual museums, Digital cultural heritage, Extended reality, User experience, Accessibility in virtual museums**

1. **Introduction**

Digital cultural heritage initiatives are rapidly reshaping how museums preserve and share their collections. In response, the Anonymous Art Museum (MAM) is exploring a virtual museum as a strategic way to expand access to its archives. A virtual museum—an immersive, interactive digital environment—can broaden educational, cultural, and community engagement by extending the institution into online space. This shift reflects a broader transformation in the museum field; as Parry (2007) states, “the museum is no longer merely a building, but a distributed network of digital experiences,” emphasizing the growing importance of digital strategies for cultural institutions.

Digital participation is now central to museum practice. Simon (2010) emphasizes that “participatory cultural institutions invite users to engage, create, and share,” highlighting the need for user-centered, interactive design in virtual environments. Economou and Meintani (2021) argue that “virtual museums have become essential tools for promoting heritage and expanding audience access,” demonstrating their growing relevance in contemporary museum ecosystems. Likewise, Bruno et al. (2020) note that “3D digitization and visualization are now fundamental components of cultural heritage documentation,” underscoring the importance of integrating advanced imaging technologies into MAM’s strategy. From a user experience perspective, Hazan and Hermon (2021) assert that “evaluating user experience in virtual museums is critical to ensuring meaningful engagement,” a finding that directly informs the project’s methodological emphasis on iterative testing and refinement.

This research proposal outlines a comprehensive strategy for developing a virtual museum platform that enables global audiences to explore MAM’s archival materials while supporting long-term preservation, educational outreach, and community engagement. The project is guided by key research questions: What technical and curatorial frameworks best support a virtual museum for MAM’s archives? How can virtual exhibitions enhance public engagement and learning outcomes? What accessibility and inclusive standards should guide the platform’s design? How can MAM sustain and expand the virtual museum over time?

The primary objective in this research is to design and evaluate an implementation strategy for a virtual museum that effectively exhibits MAM’s archival collections, with the Brook Stevens collection as an initial phase. Specific goals include developing a scalable digital infrastructure for archiving and presenting materials; creating interactive, user-centered exhibition experiences; ensuring accessibility for diverse audiences, including remote learners and individuals with disabilities; establishing workflows for ongoing digitization and XR implementation, curation, and platform maintenance; and measuring user engagement, learning outcomes, and community impact.

Research Objectives

* Develop a scalable digital infrastructure for presenting MAM’s archival materials.  
* Create interactive, user-centered virtual exhibitions.  
* Establish digitization, metadata, and preservation workflows.  
* Evaluate user engagement, accessibility, and educational impact.  
* Produce a long-term implementation strategy for museum leadership.

2. **Methodology**

This project is developed through an Anonymous Preservation Institute in collaboration with Anonymous University and an Anonymous business partner. It uses a mixed methods User Experience (UX) and Extended Reality (XR) research framework consisting of six stages: (1) conducting stakeholder interviews to assess needs; (2) analyzing leading virtual museum models; (3) designing a technical framework that incorporates 3D environments, metadata standards, and accessibility requirements; (4) developing a functional prototype; (5) testing the prototype with diverse audiences; and (6) evaluating results using qualitative and quantitative methods. The paper presents findings from the first four stages.

The research employs a mixed-methods approach:  
Needs Assessment: Conduct interviews with MAM staff, educators, and community stakeholders to identify priorities, technical constraints, and desired user experiences.  
Comparative Analysis: Examine virtual museum models from institutions such as the Smithsonian, the British Museum, and the Van Gogh Museum to identify best practices in interface design, metadata standards, and digital storytelling.  
Technical Framework Development: Propose a platform architecture integrating 3D gallery environments, high-resolution imaging, metadata management, and accessibility features.  
Prototype Creation: Develop a functional prototype featuring a curated selection of archival materials, incorporating interactive elements such as zoomable images, audio narration, and thematic pathways.  
User Testing: Recruit participants from local schools, community groups, and general audiences to evaluate usability, engagement, and educational value.  
Data Analysis: Use qualitative feedback and quantitative metrics to refine the implementation strategy.  
**1\. Needs Assessment**

* Interviews with MAM curators, archivists, educators, and IT staff.

* Surveys of community members, educators, and museum visitors.

**2\. Comparative Case Studies**

* Analysis of virtual museum models from the Smithsonian, British Museum, Van Gogh Museum, and others.

* Identification of best practices in metadata standards, 3D environments, and user experience.

**3\. Technical Framework Development**

* Selection of platform architecture (e.g., WebXR, Unity, Omeka S, or custom CMS).

* Development of metadata schema aligned with Dublin Core and IIIF standards.

* Integration of accessibility features (screen readers, alt text, captioning, adjustable contrast).

**4\. Prototype Development**

* Digitization of a pilot collection (e.g., early Milwaukee artists, architectural drawings, or photography archives).

* Creation of a 3D gallery or interactive interface.

* Incorporation of multimedia elements such as audio guides, curator commentary, and thematic pathways.

**5\. User Testing & Evaluation**

* Usability testing with educators, students, and general audiences.

* Metrics: engagement time, navigation patterns, learning outcomes, accessibility feedback.

* Iterative refinement based on findings.

Anticipated outcomes include increased public access to MAM’s archives, enhanced educational resources for regional schools, and strengthened community engagement. **2\. Literature Review (Condensed)**  
**Digital Museums & Virtual Access**  
Research consistently shows that virtual museums expand access and enhance learning. Marty (2008) argues that digital platforms “extend the museum’s mission beyond its walls,” enabling broader participation. Virtual exhibitions also support multimodal learning, allowing users to explore content through text, audio, video, and interactive elements.  
**User Engagement & Interactivity**  
Nina Simon’s *The Participatory Museum* (2010) emphasizes that interactive digital experiences foster deeper engagement by allowing users to contribute, interpret, and personalize their experience. Studies show that virtual environments increase dwell time and improve recall of information.  
**Digitization & Metadata Standards**  
Digitization literature highlights the importance of consistent metadata for discoverability and preservation. Standards such as Dublin Core, IIIF, and linked-data frameworks ensure interoperability across institutions.  
**Accessibility & Inclusive Design**  
Research from the W3C Web Accessibility Initiative demonstrates that accessible digital design expands audience reach and improves usability for all users. Museums adopting universal design principles report higher engagement from visitors with disabilities.  
**Sustainability & Institutional Capacity**  
Scholars note that virtual museum projects succeed when institutions plan for long-term maintenance, staff training, and iterative updates. Sustainable digital heritage requires ongoing investment in infrastructure and skills.

3. **Discussion**

The discussion focuses on major themes shaping digital heritage work today: the role of virtual museums in expanding cultural access; the balance between technological innovation and curatorial integrity; long term sustainability of digital heritage; accessibility as a foundational design principle; and user experience as a key driver of engagement. The project aims to produce an evidence based strategy for launching a virtual museum that increases public access to MAM’s archives, enhances digital engagement, strengthens educational resources, and deepens community connections. Ultimately, the initiative positions MAM as a leader in digital cultural heritage with both regional and broader **impact**.

•  The evolving role of virtual museums in democratizing cultural access: How does the shift from physical to digital spaces reshape who gets to engage with art, archives, and cultural memory?

•  Balancing technological innovation with curatorial integrity: In what ways can advanced 3D digitization, UX/XR design, and virtual storytelling enhance—or potentially distort—the interpretive work of museums?

•  Sustainability and long term stewardship of digital heritage: What institutional, financial, and technical frameworks are required to ensure that virtual museums remain accessible, relevant, and preserved over time?

•  Accessibility as a core design principle rather than an add on: How can virtual museum platforms meaningfully integrate inclusive design to serve diverse audiences, including those historically excluded from physical museum spaces?

•  User experience as a driver of cultural engagement: How do participatory, interactive, and immersive digital environments transform visitor expectations and redefine what it means to “visit” a museum?
