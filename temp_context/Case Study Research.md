**Name:** Votes for Women: A Portrait of Persistence

**Designer:** Smithsonian’s National Portrait Gallery

**Date:** March 29, 2019

**Link:** [https://artsandculture.google.com/story/ogWxZX3r7pZoJA](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/ogWxZX3r7pZoJA)

**Description:** “Votes for Women” is typical of most of the Smithsonian’s virtual exhibitions, utilizing Google Art & Culture to create a slideshow-esque presentation where the user scrolls through a series of images and descriptions. The exhibition is divided chronologically and thematically to tell the story of the American women’s suffrage movement between 1832 and 1920, and “seeks to tell a more complete story of the movement through portraits of women who represent different races, ages, and fields of endeavor.”

| Pros: | Cons: |
| :---- | :---- |
| Ample space for detailed descriptions Images occupy the whole screen Ability to click on "ⓘ” symbol for more detailed information about the image Easy to navigate, no tutorial needed Blurry, non-distracting backgrounds Web format allows for use of accessibility aids Slideshow format creates a simple linear narrative, fitting for a historical timeline Slideshow forces users to view images in the intended order Link to other exhibitions at the end Formatted for desktop and mobile Can "favorite” to add to Google Arts & Culture profile | Slideshow format is passive and unengaging Have to hover over "ⓘ” symbol for citation which is not always visible No ability to zoom into images No built-in accessibility options (text-to-speech, ability to adjust font or contrast) Needs Wi-Fi to view (no download); images don’t appear under slow Wi-Fi Text covers images in many cases No freedom in the order or manner to view images Can only view one image at a time On desktop, sometimes cuts off text |

**Name:** Americans

**Designer:** Smithsonian’s National Museum of the American Indian

**Date:** January 18, 2018

**Link:** [https://americanindian.si.edu/americans/\#gallery](https://americanindian.si.edu/americans/#gallery) 

**Description:** “Americans” is an online exhibit illustrating the variety of impacts that Native Americans have had on American culture throughout time. It is arranged as a “drag and click” gallery comprised of art, objects, and photographs that the user can click on to learn more about. It also includes 3 “stories” that are scrolling web pages that dive deeper into specific topics as well as a curator-narrated video.

| Pros: | Cons: |
| :---- | :---- |
| High degree of freedom for the user; can control the order to view images and stories The click and drag navigation feels more engaging than scrolling Sleek design, non-distracting background, legible font Stories and image pages allow for ample text descriptions Multiple learning formats (images, text, video) Includes Instagram and Twitter links Appropriate background music | Requires a higher bandwidth than more traditional slideshow presentations Images in gallery are very small No information about each image until you click on them Stories resort to scrolling for navigation, very linear and unengaging Hard to locate a specific image Can’t zoom in in the gallery No embedded accessibility features (text-to-speech, font adjustment, metatext, etc.) Needs Wi-FI (no download) |

**Name:** National Museum of Natural History Virtual Tours

**Designer:** Smithsonian’s National Museum of Natural History; Imagery and coding by Loren Ybarrondo

**Date:** n/a

**Link:** [https://naturalhistory2.si.edu/vt3/NMNH/?startscene=19\&startactions=lookat(-30.9,-3.6,120,0,0](https://naturalhistory2.si.edu/vt3/NMNH/?startscene=19&startactions=lookat\(-30.9,-3.6,120,0,0)); 

**Description:** The Virtual Tour of the Smithsonian Institute created through 360 imagery of the museum itself that seeks to imitate an actual visit to the museum. Users navigate by clicking blue arrows along the ground, or by selecting destinations from a side panel. Each exhibit has a blue number hovering over it that the user can click on to see up-close imagery, text descriptions, and occasional videos providing deeper insights.

| Pros: | Cons: |
| :---- | :---- |
| VR capabilities High degree of freedom for the user, like being within the museum itself Labels over exhibits Pop-ups with more information and higher quality images of each exhibit Navigation menus along bottom and sidebar Full-screen mode Similar to Google Earth navigation, likely to be known by users Map and view cone of floorplan | Hand-coded means high amount of maintenance and limited replicability Fish-eye effect when zoomed out Needs Wi-Fi (no download) Must click on arrows to move camera, not total freedom Buttons might distract from exhibits |

**Notes:**

Equipment Used: Professional Nikon digital single-lens reflex (DSLR) camera bodies and lenses. The photography is typically done using rectilinear lenses with minimized distortion and shooting equirectangular panoramas at 22K pixels on the long side.

Software Used: No authoring software is used. The tours are hand-coded in HTML5 and JavaScript using the krpano graphics library.

**Name:** Artemisia Gentileschi: an immersive experience

**Designer:** Gallerie d’Italia, curated by Intesa Sanparolo, realized by Punta Rec Studios

**Date:** n/a

**Link:** [https://gallerieditalia.com/en/online-museums/virtual-tour/artemisia-gentileschi-an-immersive-experience/](https://gallerieditalia.com/en/online-museums/virtual-tour/artemisia-gentileschi-an-immersive-experience/) 

**Description:** This virtual experience is dedicated to the Italian painter Artemisia Gentileschi during her stay in Naples. The experience sets the user in a virtual room surrounded by ink drawings of 17th\-century Naples with cutouts of scenes from her artwork as well as floating paintings placed around the room. The user can click on each cutout to find audio and video stories (accompanied by written text) about the artwork.

| Pros: | Cons: |

**Name:** National Gallery Imaginarium

**Designer:** Developed by Fabrique and Q42

**Date:** n/a

**Link:** [https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/visiting/virtual-gallery/national-gallery-imaginarium](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/visiting/virtual-gallery/national-gallery-imaginarium) 

**Description:** The National Gallery Imaginarium is a fantastical virtual experience that “combines soundscapes, guided interaction, poetry and animations, to help you pause and see your paintings afresh.” It begins with a poem written and narrated by Sir Ben Okri, then brings the user into a whimsical gallery full of floating paintings with an ethereal soundscape. | Pros: | Cons: |

**Name:** The Director's Choice

**Designer:** The National Gallery, curated by Dr Gabriele Finaldi

**Date:** 2021

**Link:** [https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/visiting/virtual-tours/the-director-s-choice](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/visiting/virtual-tours/the-director-s-choice) 

**Description:** “The Director’s Choice” is set in a virtual museum room containing a selection of the museum director’s favorite artworks from the National Gallery. **Name:** Dreams of Dalí

**Designer:** The Dalí Museum

**Date:** April 17, 2019

**Link:** [https://thedali.org/exhibit/dreams-of-dali-in-virtual-reality/](https://thedali.org/exhibit/dreams-of-dali-in-virtual-reality/) 

**Description:** “Dream of Dalí” is an immersive VR experience recreating Dali’s painting Archaeological Reminiscence of Millet’s “Angelus.” While it is designed to be viewed on an Oculus Rift or HTC Vive, the Dalí Museum also provides a Youtube 360 video that can be viewed on simpler VR devices like a Google Cardboard or simply within the web browser. It reimagines Dalí’s painting as a surreal, sculptural 3D environment complete with a custom soundscape.

| Pros: | Cons: |
| :---- | :---- |
| Highly immersive and artistic Unique and memorable, creates an experience not just a presentation of the art Fitting soundscape Full 360° view, something to be seen in every direction While VR, can be viewed from more accessible devices Allows users to experience something offered in the museum from home Can zoom Uses a well known and supported platform, Youtube  | No dialogue or text, no real education beyond the aesthetic experience Requires experience hardware for the intended experience Requires a good Wi-Fi connection or else images are blurry Limited freedom of movement, constrained to one path No explanations for the visuals presented Clunky to navigate with a trackpad |
