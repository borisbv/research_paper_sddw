The migratory phenomenon in Chile has undergone a structural transformation by the year 2024, characterized by a staggering increase in the housing deficit and the emergence of informal settlements that challenge the National Migration and Foreigners Policy (PNME) 2024-2025. This article reports on the design and validation of the "Casa de paso" (Halfway House) prototype, a transitional housing solution based on User Experience (UX) methodology and universal design principles for vulnerable populations in irregular situations. Through exploratory qualitative research involving 44 migrants and 90 architecture students, a five-stage UX path (Empathize, Research, Design, Prototype, and Test) was applied to capture user voices and translate them into technical housing requirements. Findings revealed three critical categories for dignified habitability: the notion of **temporary home** as a space for emotional connection, the efficiency of geodesic **modular housing**, and the integration of **civic life** through common spaces that foster social fabric. Results demonstrate that humanitarian architecture, when articulated with a person-centered approach, not only satisfies the basic need for shelter but also acts as a tool for dignification and resistance against systematic exclusion. The study concludes that it is imperative to align these architectural innovations with public urban integration policies to transition from emergency response toward sustainable and inclusive settlement models in the Latin American context.
