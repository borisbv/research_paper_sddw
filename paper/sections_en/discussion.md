The discussion of findings reveals that the design of the "Casa de paso" transitional housing transcends a mere technical response to the housing emergency, situating itself at a critical intersection between humanitarian architecture and public policies for migrant integration in Chile. In this regard, the National Migration and Foreigners Policy (PNME) 2024-2025, formalized through **Decree 181** \cite{PNME2024}, prioritizes security and control measures, such as biometric registration for persons who entered through unauthorized crossings. While these actions seek identification and management of administrative backlogs, the manuscript reveals an operational void in the "Housing and Habitability" axis: current policy lacks technical guidelines for creating transitional reception infrastructures, delegating habitability responsibility to structurally overwhelmed municipalities and civil society organizations (serviciomigraciones.cl, 2024).

The results of this study suggest that User Experience (UX) methodology offers an effective operational framework to bridge the gap between migratory control and the realities lived by the population in irregular situations. This strategic alignment allows the "Casa de paso" prototype to be interpreted not as a marginal intervention, but as a technical infrastructure capable of operationalizing the humanitarian regularization mentioned in Decree 181. Ávila Martínez (2022) highlights that access to shelter is directly linked to the reconstruction of dignity and autonomy for displaced persons, facilitating the transition toward resilient urban integration. However, the persistence of **biometric registration** as a control measure may act as a psychological barrier to full appropriation of these spaces, unless architecture is designed, as in this case, under principles of trust and emotional connection.

Furthermore, the research underscores the importance of considering architecture not only as a physical object but as a communication and emotional resilience device. The persistence of cultural elements—such as the use of warm colors and centralized coexistence spaces—acts as a tool of resistance against the hostility of host territories and socio-cultural stigmatization. However, the effectiveness of these prototypes is limited by the existing disconnection between academic innovations and Chilean State housing policies, which, as Tapia et al. (2020) warn, tend to prioritize market solutions that often perpetuate socio-spatial segregation. The integration of universal design and sustainability principles in the "Casa de paso" demonstrates that it is feasible to develop high technical quality infrastructures that are, at the same time, inclusive and sensitive to the human context. Therefore, it is imperative that current regulatory frameworks transcend the emergency phase and foster the creation of comprehensive reception environments that value cultural diversity as an asset for sustainable urban development.

In conclusion, the prototype designed through the UX path is positioned as a coherent response to the migrant housing crisis, based on the notion of **home** as the axis of transitional habitability. The research demonstrated that active user participation in design allows for the development of solutions that not only satisfy functional requirements but also strengthen the emotional connection and sense of belonging of vulnerable communities. The replicability of this model in other global migratory crisis contexts represents an opportunity to transform emergency architecture into an engine for social integration and human dignity. It is recommended that future Chilean public housing policies incorporate user-centered design methodologies to ensure that transitional housing solutions cease to be spaces of exclusion and become true catalysts for inclusion and well-being for all persons inhabiting the national territory, regardless of their administrative status.
