The staggering increase in the number of forcibly displaced persons has reconfigured global socio-demographic dynamics, reaching a historical high of 108.4 million external displacements by the year 2022 \cite{ACNUR2022}. Following this trend, Latin America and the Caribbean have recorded a 52% increase in human mobility, positioning Chile as one of the primary regional receptors due to its relative economic stability and quality of life \cite{UN2017}. By 2020, the Chilean territory hosted 1,645,015 immigrants, representing 8.45% of the total population, with a predominance of Venezuelan, Peruvian, and Haitian communities that have integrated diversely into the social fabric \cite{DEP2021}. However, this massive exodus, exacerbated by irregular entries and structural vulnerability factors, poses critical challenges for national public policies, which must guarantee the human rights of the migrant population while fostering effective integration into the host society \cite{Barahona2022}.

Sánchez-Montijano (2022) and Galán Crespo (2023) argue that integration into the host country emerges as a multidimensional process that transcends mere administrative regularization, involving linguistic barriers, socio-cultural stigmatization, and, fundamentally, access to basic services such as health, education, and housing. The latest National Camp Registry (2022-2023) evidence a sustained growth of informal settlements, where 34.74% of resident families are migrants, many of them in irregular situations \cite{TECHO2023}. This phenomenon is closely linked to the worsening of post-pandemic economic conditions and precarious employment, factors that push migrants toward informal housing solutions due to legal barriers to accessing the formal rental market \cite{Avila2022, Tapia2020}. The lack of a coherent housing response from the State has exacerbated the vulnerability of these groups, who are often excluded from fundamental guarantees of security and dignified habitability.

Faced with this problematic, this article explores housing expectations and the functional characteristics that a transitional housing solution should possess for the migrant population in irregular situations. The central objective is the design of the "Casa de paso" prototype, conceived as a humanitarian architecture response for emergency or social risk environments. To achieve this purpose, the results of applying qualitative tools designed from a *User Experience* (UX) perspective are reported, which valued direct user information to understand their behaviors and needs \cite{Aguirre2021, Aguirre2023}. The methodology employed not only seeks architectural functionality but also integrates sustainability principles, using recyclable materials and easy assembly techniques that minimally impact the natural environment \cite{Espinosa2021, FerrerMavarez2023}. Through this person-centered approach, the study proposes a model of dignified living that dialogues with the recent National Migration and Foreigners Policy (PNME) 2024-2025, seeking to bridge the gap between housing emergency and effective social integration.
