The development of the transitional housing proposal was based on exploratory research with a descriptive design, articulated through interdisciplinary collaboration between a Chilean public university with technological specialization and technical advice from a public research university in the United States. The study sample consisted of forty-four migrants in irregular situations, corresponding to eight families residing in the Santiago and Tarapacá regions, along with ninety architecture students organized into twenty-two work teams. The recruitment process was facilitated by the Jesuit Migrant and Refugee Service, ensuring a bond of trust with the participating communities. The main methodological strategy focused on User Experience (UX) methodology, supported by the *Design Thinking* approach to prioritize accessibility, usability, and final user satisfaction in the architectural design \cite{MartinezPadua2022, Aguirre2021}. This conceptual framework allowed for the integration of the migrant population's voice in each phase of the project, ensuring that the final prototype responded to the socio-cultural and housing realities detected in the field.

The implementation of the UX path was structured sequentially through five critical stages that allowed for transitioning from abstract understanding of needs to empirical validation of the architectural product. In this sense, the initial phase of Empathize (*UX Empathy*) focused on deep exploration of the integration and exclusion realities experienced by immigrants in Chile through semi-structured interviews. Following this progression, the Research stage (*UX Research*) analyzed the collected qualitative data to define housing expectations and the geographical adaptability of the prototype, using tools such as user journey mapping and comparative benchmarking. Subsequently, in the Design phase (*UX Design*), visual inspiration canvases or moodboards were generated, connecting the age and cultural characteristics of the participants with specific chromatic and formal proposals. Finally, the Prototyping (*UX Prototyping*) and Testing (*UX Testing*) stages allowed for the materialization of models in various fidelity levels (low, medium, and high) to evaluate their functionality and sustainability alongside relevant actors, adjusting the final design based on direct feedback from the migrant community.

Furthermore, the design process was managed under a collaborative and self-organized work model, employing development cycles known as *sprints* to ensure agility and continuous value delivery. During these defined time periods, focus groups and expert reviews were conducted to evaluate task quality and optimize workflow from the user experience \cite{Schwaber2020}. The integration of the migrant population in these phases was crucial to eliminate "waste" in the design and ensure that habitability criteria were consistent with their life stories and migratory trajectories \cite{Orozco2023}. 

Qualitative data processing was performed using **Reflexive Thematic Analysis** \cite{BraunClarke2006}, structured in six phases that allowed for transitioning from initial semantic coding to the identification of latent themes. For the management and systematization of verbatim excerpts derived from interviews and focus groups, *Atlas.ti* (v.23) qualitative analysis software was used, facilitating the organization of emerging categories (**temporary home**, **modular housing**, and **civic life**). Methodological rigor was guaranteed through a researcher triangulation process, where themes were collectively refined by the academic team and architecture students to ensure intersubjective validity of the findings. This approach not only captured users' functional needs but also revealed the emotional and symbolic dimensions that configure migrant living in contexts of transience.

The use of sustainable materials and rapid assembly techniques was transversal to the entire methodology, seeking not only a functional solution but a model that minimized environmental impact and maximized the dignity of transient residents in contexts of extreme vulnerability.
