The research and design of the "Casa de paso" transitional housing prototype demonstrate conclusively that humanitarian architecture, when grounded in direct user experience, possesses transformative potential for the social integration of migrant communities in Chile. Through the systematic application of User Experience (UX) methodology, the study successfully transitioned from identifying habitability barriers to validating a housing product that not only meets functional technical requirements but also serves as a support for emotional connection and human dignity. Results underscore that the notion of home, articulated through chromatic warmth, spatial flexibility, and the integration of civic life, is a determining factor for the success of temporary housing solutions in contexts of extreme vulnerability. In this sense, the geodesic prototype not only offers physical shelter but symbolizes a commitment to radical inclusion that challenges the logic of segregation and precariousness prevailing in the informal housing market.

Finally, the study highlights the imperative need to align these architectural innovations with more ambitious and human mobility-sensitive public policy frameworks. The emerging guidelines of the National Migration and Foreigners Policy (PNME) 2024-2025 represent a significant advance but need to be complemented by housing strategies that value cultural diversity and universal design as pillars of social cohesion. The replicability of the UX methodology and the "Casa de paso" prototype in other latitudes suggests a horizon of hope for the management of global migratory crises, where architecture ceases to be a reactive response to become an engine for human development and urban justice. It is fundamental, therefore, that academic and state agendas converge in creating reception infrastructures that recognize migrants as subjects of rights, capable of contributing to and redefining destination territories through their dignified and participatory living.
